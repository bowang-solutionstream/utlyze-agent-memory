# Post-run engineering brief

Show the actual result and readiness first. Immediately follow it with a brief that takes roughly two to three minutes to read, scaled to the work. Choose the one to three decisions with the most transferable value.

For each selected decision, explain:

1. The problem, relevant input → boundary → output path, and the invariant protected. Define an unfamiliar concept before using it.
2. The actual choice and why responsibility belongs there.
3. The alternatives actually considered and why they were not selected under the task's constraints.
4. The complexity, coupling, latency or maintenance burden accepted, and what evidence would justify reconsideration.
5. The failure and verification evidence, including what remains unproved.
6. One reusable engineering principle with a tiny example from the actual run.

Use source, decision and proof references. An example must agree with the actual behavior or be clearly labeled illustrative. A newly suggested comparison is retrospective, not an alternative the author considered. If alternatives or a verified cause were not recorded, say so. A diagram is useful only when it clarifies a boundary or flow.

Keep raw logs private and link detailed evidence instead of copying it. The brief cannot grant release approval or turn incomplete proof into completion. For failed/interrupted work, explain what is known, uncertainty and the next investigation. No quiz or reply is required for handoff.

Record the concepts presented and evidence references in the private run ledger. Keep later owner corrections append-only. Presentation is not evidence of understanding or mastery; the record must not invent a learner score or treat silence as consent.
